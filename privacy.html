Revisit Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 7, 2026. Applies to Revisit 0.3.0.

Publisher: ob2. Public contact: hquyenhoang26@duck.com. Revisit is independent and is not affiliated with or endorsed by X or Google.

Purpose and consent

Revisit resurfaces one of your locally collected X bookmarks on X Home, prioritizes posts you have seen least, opens the original post and its replies, and lets you remove or restore that bookmark through an explicit action.

A new installation presents an in-product collection disclosure and requires your explicit agreement before collection or saved-content display begins. It does not ask a new user to choose a local password. Your agreement is remembered, and normal use continues without a password prompt after Chrome restarts. You do not need a Revisit account, X developer application, API key or paid API.

Before agreement, Revisit does not read your X account for collection, copy or parse bookmark responses, collect rendered bookmark posts, or show saved post content.

Information handled

After agreement, Revisit handles the visible X username; bookmark and post identifiers; post text and dates; author names, usernames, profile identifiers and avatar references; media links and alternative text; available reply, ordinary repost, quote, like and view totals; bookmark state; display counts; collection times; and settings. Engagement totals are the last collected snapshot, not continuously refreshed counters.

Collection covers bookmarks X loads during your normal Bookmarks browsing, including the base History/bookmarks route. Likes and unrelated History sections are excluded. Revisit does not automatically scroll, traverse unloaded pages or claim to retrieve your complete historical library. Responses with an unknown or changed account are discarded. Records are separated by the X account shown on the page.

Only when migrating an older 0.2.0 password-protected store, Revisit may process the old local password you enter. It is used on your device to open the old store for migration, is not retained, and is not sent to X or the publisher. This is not a new-password requirement and is separate from your X password.

Automatic local encryption and its limits

Collected posts, account/post relationships, selection data and display counts are encrypted locally with AES-256-GCM. Revisit generates a device key and stores it as a non-extractable CryptoKey in local IndexedDB within the same Chrome profile as the encrypted records. It can use this key automatically after a restart, without asking for a password.

Non-extractable means that normal WebCrypto export operations do not return the key's raw bytes. It does not mean the key is kept outside this profile or that code with access to the stored key cannot use it. This automatic local encryption does not provide the same protection as a separately held password. Revisit does not claim to protect against someone who can read or copy the entire Chrome profile, malware, a compromised browser or another person using the profile.

This is a device-local storage choice, not a statement that Chrome requires a password. No new local password is created, saved or uploaded by the 0.3.0 setup.

Retention, upgrade and deletion

Encrypted records and the device key remain in this Chrome profile after the browser closes. Revisit does not synchronize them to other devices. Logging out of X does not erase the local store.

For an existing 0.2.0 password-protected store, a valid previously unlocked session can allow automatic migration. Otherwise the extension asks for the old local password once to migrate the saved posts and display counts to device-key storage. After successful migration, normal use and browser restarts do not require that password. If the old password is unavailable and the old store cannot be opened, it cannot be recovered by Revisit; you can instead clear local data and recollect bookmarks from X.

Earlier unencrypted development records are migrated only after explicit collection agreement. Old records are removed through the browser's storage APIs after successful migration. This is logical deletion, not a promise of forensic erasure of disk blocks or backups.

Clear local data deletes the local encrypted posts, display records, device key, consent and older local records. It cancels pending helper tabs and returns the extension to the first-consent state. It does not delete your bookmarks on X. After you agree again, newly collected posts start with new display records. Uninstalling also removes extension-local data.

Removing a bookmark through Revisit excludes it from future selection after a confirmed result. Its encrypted content and previous display count are retained so restoring it can preserve history. Changes made elsewhere, deleted/edited posts and access changes may not be reflected until relevant X content is loaded again. The local archive is not a guarantee that an original post remains available.

X login and explicit bookmark actions

Revisit does not ask for or read your X password, Cookie values, authorization headers or anti-forgery headers. It does not generate transaction identifiers or reconstruct authenticated bookmark-write requests.

An explicit bookmark click on the Home card briefly opens the original post in a background X tab, verifies the matching account/post and uses X's own bookmark button. That tab closes after completion, cancellation or failure. The browser sends its ordinary X login cookies with native website requests. The original-post visit is visible to X. Revisit updates Home state only after a confirmed result.

Clearing local data cancels pending helper tabs and stops further collection until renewed agreement. An action already sent to X may still complete on X; local clearing cannot revoke a request the website has already received. If a result is not confirmed, verify the original post rather than assuming it changed or stayed unchanged.

Network connections and sharing

Revisit has no bookmark backend, cloud bookmark account, advertising, analytics or tracking system. The publisher does not receive the local bookmark database or device key. Avatar and media references can load content from X or its content-delivery providers. Opening originals, reply/repost flows, like/share entry points and explicit helper visits use X's website and its policies. Media references are not an offline download.

All extension logic is bundled. It does not download executable extension code, evaluate remote strings as code or load further bookmark pages automatically. Relevant responses are treated as data only after collection is allowed.

The public privacy/support website uses GitHub Pages, with an additional Sites deployment. Website visits can involve ordinary hosting access information such as IP address and browser details; this is separate from the extension's local bookmark store. Its source includes no application analytics, advertising scripts, bookmark upload form or login requirement.

If you email support, the email services delivering and answering the request process your contact address and message. Do not send old passwords, cookies, session tokens or private bookmark archives. Share specific support material only if you deliberately choose to do so.

Controls

The Home display switch controls the card only; it does not stop collection after agreement. Close bookmark pages or disable/uninstall the extension to stop further collection. Clear local data revokes the stored consent, and nothing is recollected until you explicitly agree again. There is no routine password-unlock step or password-based lock mode in 0.3.0.

Revisit depends on X's current page structure and native buttons. X changes or restrictions can affect operation. Functional tests, encryption and local operation do not establish X permission or Chrome Web Store approval.

Limited Use commitment

Revisit's use and transfer of user data adheres to the Limited Use requirements of the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy. Data is used only for the disclosed bookmark collection, resurfacing and management feature. It is not sold, transferred for advertising, used for credit decisions or unrelated profiling. The publisher does not receive the bookmark database and does not allow people to read users' local bookmark content. Specific information deliberately sent for support is handled only for that request.
