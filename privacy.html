Revisit Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 7, 2026. Applies to Revisit 0.2.0.

Publisher: ob2. Public contact: hquyenhoang26@duck.com. Revisit is independent and is not affiliated with or endorsed by X or Google.

Purpose and consent

Revisit resurfaces one of your locally collected X bookmarks on X Home, prioritizes posts you have seen least, opens the original post and its replies, and lets you remove or restore that bookmark through an explicit action.

Before collection starts, Revisit presents an in-product disclosure and asks you to choose a local password and select “Agree and protect my bookmarks.” Until you agree and unlock the local store, it does not read your X account for collection, copy or parse bookmark responses, collect rendered bookmark posts, or show saved post content. You do not need a Revisit account, X developer application, API key or paid API.

Information handled

After your agreement, Revisit handles the visible X username; bookmark and post identifiers; post text and dates; author names, usernames, profile identifiers and avatar references; media links and alternative text; available reply, ordinary repost, quote, like and view totals; bookmark state; local display counts; collection times; and settings. Engagement totals are the last collected snapshot, not continuously refreshed counters.

Collection is limited to bookmarks that X loads while you normally browse its Bookmarks view, including the base History/bookmarks route. Likes and unrelated History sections are excluded. Revisit does not automatically scroll, traverse unloaded pages or claim to retrieve your complete historical library. Responses with an unknown or changed account are discarded. Records are separated by the X account shown on the page.

Revisit also handles the local password you enter in its own popup to derive an encryption key on your device. It does not send that password to X, ob2 or a bookmark server, and it does not store the password. This local password is separate from your X password.

Encryption and session access

Persistent posts, account/post relationships, selection indexes, settings and display counts are encrypted locally with AES-256-GCM. A password-derived key uses PBKDF2-SHA256 with 600,000 iterations and a random salt. Encryption uses random 96-bit nonces and authenticated context. Post records use random storage identifiers rather than plaintext X account or post identifiers.

The password is not retained. The derived key is kept only in extension memory and Chrome storage.session, restricted to trusted extension contexts. That session storage is not written as a persistent key by Revisit. It lets the extension service worker resume during the same browser session without repeatedly asking for the password. After Chrome restarts or you select Lock Revisit, the local password is required again. The key is not placed next to the encrypted database as a persistent plaintext key.

Encryption protects persisted records when the password and unlocked session are unavailable. It is not a guarantee against malware, a compromised browser or someone using an already-unlocked Chrome session. Revisit cannot recover a forgotten local password. Clearing local data resets the store; your bookmarks on X remain available.

Retention, migration and deletion

Encrypted records remain in this Chrome profile after the browser closes. Revisit does not synchronize them to other devices. Logging out of X does not erase the local store.

Earlier development builds stored records without application encryption. The upgraded extension does not display or collect those records before setup. When you explicitly agree and set a password, it migrates the old posts and display history into encrypted storage and deletes the old records accessible through the browser's storage APIs. You may instead clear local data to discard them without migration. Deletion is logical removal from browser storage; it does not promise forensic erasure of old disk blocks or backups.

Use Clear local data in the popup to delete the encrypted posts, display records, consent state and encryption setup, remove any older local records, and forget the session key. The confirmation explains that X bookmarks are unaffected. Uninstalling also removes extension-local data. Newly collected posts start with new display records after a reset.

Removing a bookmark through Revisit excludes it from future selection after a confirmed result. Its encrypted local content and previous display count are retained so restoring it can preserve history. Changes made elsewhere, deleted/edited posts and access changes may not be reflected until relevant X content is loaded again. The local archive is not a guarantee that an original post remains available.

X login and explicit bookmark actions

Revisit does not ask for or read your X password, Cookie values, authorization headers or anti-forgery headers. It does not generate transaction identifiers or reconstruct authenticated bookmark-write requests.

An explicit bookmark click on the Home card briefly opens the original post in a background X tab, verifies the matching account/post and uses X's own bookmark button. That tab closes after completion, cancellation or failure. The browser sends its ordinary X login cookies with native website requests. The original-post visit is visible to X. Revisit updates its Home state only after a confirmed result.

Locking or clearing cancels pending helper tabs and stops further collection. An action already sent to X may still complete on X; local locking cannot revoke a request that the website has already received. If a result is not confirmed, verify the original post rather than assuming it was changed or unchanged.

Network connections and sharing

Revisit has no bookmark backend, cloud bookmark account, advertising, analytics or tracking system. The publisher does not receive your local bookmark database. Avatar and media references can load content from X or its content-delivery providers. Opening originals, reply/repost flows, like/share entry points and explicit bookmark helper visits use X's website and its own policies. Media references are not an offline download.

All extension logic is bundled. It does not download executable extension code, evaluate remote strings as code or load further bookmark pages automatically. Relevant website responses are treated as data only after collection is allowed.

The public information and privacy/support website is hosted using GitHub Pages, with an additional Sites deployment. Visiting that website can involve ordinary hosting access information such as IP address and browser details; it is separate from the extension's encrypted bookmark store. The website source includes no application analytics, advertising scripts, bookmark upload form or login requirement.

If you email support, the email services delivering and answering the request process the contact address and message you choose to send. Do not send passwords, cookies, session tokens or private bookmark archives. Share specific support material only if you deliberately choose to do so.

Controls

Lock Revisit stops collection and hides saved content while keeping encrypted records. Unlocking resumes collection for future bookmark loads; refresh the Bookmarks page if necessary. The Home display switch only controls the Home card and does not stop collection while unlocked. Clear local data revokes the stored consent and resets the password setup. Close bookmark pages or disable/uninstall the extension to stop its collection entirely.

Revisit depends on X's current page structure and native buttons. X changes or restrictions may affect operation. Functional testing, encryption and local operation do not establish X permission or Chrome Web Store approval.

Limited Use commitment

Revisit's use and transfer of user data adheres to the Limited Use requirements of the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy. Data is used only to provide the disclosed bookmark collection, resurfacing and management feature. It is not sold, transferred for advertising, used for credit decisions or used for unrelated profiling. The publisher does not receive the bookmark database and does not allow people to read users' local bookmark content. Specific information you deliberately send for support is handled only for that support request.
